# Paper patch: Theorem 1 (restated) and Table III (regenerated)

Labels used below follow the honesty rule: **exact analytic** (computed from the model, no randomness),
**simulated** (full `bb84_numpy` protocol, saved result file), nothing here is measured on hardware.
Source files: `docs/validation/table3_theorem1.json` (data), `docs/validation/table3.tex` (tables generated by
`scripts/render_table3.py`), code in `evefl/quantum/theory.py`, tests in `tests/test_theory.py`.
Headline setting: n = 1024, sample fraction 0.25 (a documented deviation from the pre-registered rule, docs/06).

## What was wrong with the old Theorem 1 and Table III
- The old theorem used a fixed-sample-size normal approximation whose sigma (~0.031) matched a 128-bit sample
  while the text said |I| = 512. The sample size is random (the sifted length is Binomial(n, 1/2)), and the
  threshold is an integer count, so the probability is an exact mixture, not a fixed-m formula.
- The old Table III reported a 0.8% mean QBER at alpha = 0 and standard deviations of 0.3 to 1.0%. The model has no
  noise at alpha = 0, e = 0, so the QBER is exactly 0; and the standard deviation of a 128-bit sample estimate is
  1.4% at alpha = 0.1 and 3.8% at alpha = 1, much larger than the old values.

## Restated Theorem 1 (exact analytic)
```latex
\begin{theorem}[QBER detection probability]
Let $n$ qubits be sent, a fraction $f$ of the sifted key be publicly compared, Eve intercept-resend each qubit
independently with probability $\alpha$, and the channel flip Bob's result with probability $e$ after Eve.
Every sifted bit then errs independently with probability $p=e+(1-2e)\alpha/4$. Let $S\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\tfrac12)$
be the sifted length, $M=\max(1,\lfloor fS\rfloor)$ the (random) sample size, and $K\mid M\sim\mathrm{Bin}(M,p)$ the
number of sample errors. The estimate $\hat Q=K/M$ puts a link in Lockdown iff $\hat Q\ge 0.11$, i.e.\ iff
$K\ge\lceil 0.11\,M\rceil$. Then, for one link,
\[
P_{\mathrm{link}}(\alpha,e)=\sum_{s}\binom{n}{s}2^{-n}\;
\Pr\!\big[\mathrm{Bin}(m(s),p)\ge\lceil 0.11\,m(s)\rceil\big],
\]
and for $N$ independent links the system state (maximum $\hat Q$) is Lockdown with probability
$1-(1-P_{\mathrm{link}})^{N}$.
\end{theorem}
```
Proof: direct computation (the three distributional facts are validated against the full protocol, see the
Qiskit/numpy cross-check). It is an exact identity, not a bound.

| P_link at alpha = 0.66 | 0.959 | 0.973 |

- The old claim "alpha >= 0.66 gives P(Lockdown) > 0.95" is true per link, but only just (0.959 at e = 0).
  At the system level (max over 3 independent links) it holds from alpha = 0.48.
- Do NOT call alpha = 0.44 "Lockdown": the mean QBER is 11.0% but a link is in Lockdown only 49.5% of rounds
  (system 87%). Say "the mean QBER reaches the threshold".
- Integer effect: with m = 128 sampled bits, Lockdown needs 15 errors (an effective boundary of 11.72%) and
  Caution needs 7 (5.47%); m = 100: 11 and 5; m = 256: 29 (11.33%) and 13 (5.08%).

## Limitations the sweep exposed (state as results, not caveats)
1. **Noise range.** With a static 5% Caution boundary and a 128-bit sample, controller false alarms are fine at 1%
   baseline noise (system false Caution 0.11% at n = 1024), marginal at 2% (4.6%) and unusable at 3% (25.6%). The design
   assumes a calibrated baseline noise level; baseline-relative (dynamic) thresholds are future work (verify the
   Zhang et al. reference before citing).
2. **Weak Eve.** At alpha = 0.1 (n = 1024, e = 1%) a round leaves SECURE only 40% of the time, and at alpha = 0.2
   95%. Weak interception that is missed still leaks some key bits, so the privacy-amplification length must be
   computed from the upper confidence bound of the estimated QBER (Phase 1 step 3), not from the point estimate;
   until then do not claim key secrecy against weak Eve.
3. Lockdown is not the only alarm: at e = 1% the system (3 independent links) leaves SECURE with 95% probability from
   alpha = 0.2, long before Lockdown.
